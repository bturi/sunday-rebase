    Lead story · Money

    Anthropic files to go public, listing on Nasdaq as ANT

    Anthropic filed a registration statement on Form S-1 on Monday for a proposed IPO of its Class A common stock. It plans to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol ANT, and the share count and price are not set. Zvi's reading puts trailing 12-month revenue to 30 September near $35 billion and the first-half net loss above $80 billion, about $72 billion of it from fair-value changes on convertible notes and similar instruments. He warns that some figures come second-hand from the WSJ and Bloomberg and that some tables in the copy he read were blank.

    ##Models4 stories

    Google's Gemini 4 Carbon leads the Intelligence Index at 59, with access limited to cyber defenders at first

    Google calls Gemini 4 Carbon its strongest model, with state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long-horizon abilities. Access starts with a subset of cyber defenders and widens to trusted partners through the Fairwind Program, with no date for general availability. The introductory price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, rising to $4 and $20 later. Google reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro, and Artificial Analysis lists the model first at 59.

    ##Security3 stories

    A chain of three flaws let a shared link take over ChatGPT accounts

    Parameter Labs found that a sandboxed HTML preview in ChatGPT could call the parent page's MathLive keyboard handler, which used innerHTML and so let the preview inject an unsandboxed iframe. The content security policy allowed YouTube, whose oEmbed endpoint reflected attacker JavaScript back so it ran on chatgpt.com. A malicious share link opened by a signed-in user could then pull private data or act for that user. OpenAI fixed it and paid a $5,000 bounty, which Willison considers low for the severity.

    ##Money & compute3 stories

    Google says AI Mode earns less per query, and its shares fall about 15%

    Latent Space quotes Google's Chief Business Officer on the lower monetization we were seeing in AI Mode. Its headline puts the drop at about 15%, roughly $500 billion and the worst since the 2004 IPO. The piece ties the move to the tension between AI summaries and the publisher traffic they replace, and to weaker AI-sector sentiment ahead of a possible major IPO. It adds that Google does not break out AI Mode finances in enough detail for a rigorous analysis.
