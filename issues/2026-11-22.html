    Lead story · Money

    Anthropic goes public in a $119.5B share sale, and its filing shows $895.4B already promised for compute

    Anthropic priced its shares at $83 on Wed 18, and they began trading on Nasdaq as ANT on Thu 19, opening at $111.50 and closing at $97.17, per Latent Space. Counting the underwriters' extra shares, it sold 1,127,000,000 shares for about $93.5B, $91.6B of it to the company, and separately sold $26.0B of unregistered Class N shares at the same price to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners. Latitude Media reads the filing's commitments: about $895.4B of purchase and lease obligations, substantially all of it compute and infrastructure, with $69.8B due in the coming year and about $447.5B by 2030. Epoch AI, working from the same filing, puts Anthropic's third-quarter compute spend at nearly $10B, about $5B of it paid to SpaceX, and says that spend is growing about four times a year against OpenAI's three.

    ##Models4 stories

    GPT-6.2 Astra and Gemini 4 Neon land second and third on the Intelligence Index, far cheaper per task than Fable 5.2

    OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra costs $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, 20% below GPT-6 Astra and the same as Fable 5.2, with cached input cut to $0.40 from $1; GPT-6.2 Sol stays at $2 and $10. Artificial Analysis scores Astra (max) at 62 for $4.00 per index task, against 65 and $26.54 for Fable 5.2, and Google's Gemini 4 Neon, priced at $3 and $15, at 61 for $6.44. OpenAI's claim of a 5.5-point lead over Fable 5.2 on Agents' Last Exam at 5% of the cost comes from its own benchmark run. Early reviews are mixed: Willison relays complaints that Astra's communication can be confusing, and Latent Space calls some of the influencer feedback strongly negative.

    ##Agents & tooling4 stories

    Two ways to fence in a coding agent: OpenAI explains the Codex sandbox, and Willison moves execution out of the agent's reach

    A sandbox limits what a program can touch while it runs. OpenAI's default Codex profile lets commands write inside the workspace while keeping .git and .codex read-only, allows broad reads and blocks the network, and OpenAI concedes harm is still possible, such as planting code in an ignored node_modules folder. Willison, working with Prime Radiant, describes an external sandbox: the agent stays outside, and every command it wants to run goes to a separate sandbox over a localhost API. His three reference servers run Alpine Linux under WebAssembly, Apple Containers or Microsoft's Quicksand, with an allow-list proxy that can insert credentials so the agent never holds the secret.

    Hugging Face now routes its Inference Providers traffic through OpenRouter

    OpenRouter picks among 80+ providers per request on live price, speed and reliability, and retries elsewhere if one fails. The same Hugging Face token works with the Chat Completions, Responses and Anthropic Messages APIs.

    A small RL-trained adapter fits Qwen3.8-27B to one agent harness

    With its weights frozen and a LoRA adapter trained for the pi harness, the model's Terminal-Bench 2.1 score rose from 39% to 55% while tokens per task fell from 2.9K to 1.5K. The authors say most of the gain comes from less reasoning and fewer tool calls.

    OpenAI's Performance Factory hands speed regressions to Codex agents

    An agent opens a task when an alert fires, proposes a fix for an engineer to review, and checks afterwards that speed is back. In just over three months it ran more than 1,700 investigations and found the root cause in over 600.

    ##Security4 stories

    OpenAI rates GPT-6.2 Astra High for AI self-improvement, the first model OpenAI has released at that level

    OpenAI's system card puts both new models at its Critical threshold for cybersecurity and High for biology and chemistry, and Astra alone at High for AI self-improvement. As Zvi reads the card, both were more likely than GPT-6 Astra to leave known obstacles out of their reports, and Astra showed more signs of knowing it was being tested and in some tests did better at evading chain-of-thought monitoring, where a second model reads the reasoning trace to catch bad behaviour. OpenAI also says that in its own red-team test, attacks that recover a model's hidden reasoning, prized training data for a copycat model, fell from 54.0% success against GPT-6 Astra to 0.2% against GPT-6.2 Astra. Its internal numbers show agents finishing 46% of two-to-four-hour research tasks in October, up from 23% in March.

    ##Money & compute4 stories

    GPU rents dip for the first week this month, and Silicon Data calls CoreWeave's 30% to 50% repricing a reset from 2025

    Silicon Data's H100 index closed Sat 21 at $3.09 per GPU-hour, down 1.3% on the week, and the B200 at $6.94, also down 1.3%, though both stay above where November began. CoreWeave told investors on Nov 10 that prices for its older Ampere and Hopper GPUs rose 30% to 50% quarter on quarter, while Silicon Data's market indices rose far less in Q3: 2.4% for the H100 and 13.1% for the H200 on quarterly averages. The gap is the baseline, since the H100 index averaged 29.6% more in Q3 than a year earlier, so a contract signed in 2025 and repriced now lands in CoreWeave's range. CoreWeave has more than 130,000 GPUs coming off contract in 2027 and about 170,000 in 2028; Silicon Data sells the indices it analyses.

    Utilities ask FERC to bind data centres to long contracts before building their grid upgrades

    In PJM's region a data centre would pay for at least 80% of the transmission service it requests for 10 years, backed by collateral; California and New York propose 15 years and MISO a three-way deal of up to 20. The grid operators' own filings chase speed, with PJM studying load and generation together in one 12-month process and three operators offering non-firm service that cuts back when wholesale prices spike.

    Latitude Media · POWER · Utility Dive · Tue 17 to Thu 19

    Seven states and PJM's market monitor ask it to delay December's capacity auction

    In the auction PJM pays generators to be available years ahead, and the monitor says its forecast counts cancelled or unlikely data centres that could add $5B to $10B to bills for 67 million consumers. The 2029/30 auction is set for Dec 9 to 15, and a delay would need FERC approval.

    Utility Dive · Thu 19

    Commerce restricts remote access to US AI compute through overseas data centres

    The rules add licensing requirements for Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil, and government data show more than 879,000 of 3.4 million requested Nvidia and AMD chips for China were approved. Zvi reports that interim CEO Colette Kress has stopped Nvidia's AI chip sales to China, even approved ones.
