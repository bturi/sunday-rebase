    Lead story · Models

    Google DeepMind releases Gemini 4 Argon at $2 and $10 per million tokens

    Google DeepMind made Gemini 4 Argon available on Tuesday through the API in AI Studio, to AI Ultra subscribers in Antigravity, and in the Gemini app. Launch pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, with cached input at 95% off, and Willison notes the price is set to double on 1 December. Google says Argon ranks first on the Vals Index, and that teams using it in Antigravity raised agentic code submissions by more than 35% while cutting rollbacks by nearly a third. Zvi says it is too early to know whether the model lives up to its benchmarks.

    ##Models4 stories

    Meta releases Muse Wonder, and its Full tier scores 56 on the Intelligence Index

    Meta shipped Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full on Monday. Latent Space says Wonder is on the Meta Model API and in Muse Code, Full is for Muse Code subscribers only, and Wonder is priced at $4 and $20 per million input and output tokens with a 1M context window. Artificial Analysis scores Full at 56 and Wonder at 52 at max effort, up from 48 for Muse Spark 1.3, with Claude Opus 5.5 still ahead at 58. Zvi says Meta's benchmark presentation looked suspect and that it is too early to judge.

    Meta releases the weights of Muse Spark Open, an 816B model with 42B active parameters

    Hugging Face's launch post describes a mixture of experts with a 1M token context that takes text, images, audio and video and writes text. Latent Space says Artificial Analysis scores it 39, the highest for a US open-weights model.

    Reflection puts Beam on Hugging Face under Apache 2.0

    Beam has 501B parameters with 23B active, a 1M token context, and checkpoints in BF16, FP8 and NVFP4. It ships with its own Rust based harness and day-0 support in transformers, vLLM and SGLang.

    Hugging Face · Fri 16

    Import AI 476: a new benchmark finds frontier models improving at research taste

    P-Zero Research's TasteVal reports that experimental research taste has doubled every 3.0 months since December 2025. Its best model, Opus 5.5, beats the human baseline of 24 experts with a compute multiplier of 2.30x at about 1/30 of the cost per attempt.

    Jack Clark · Mon 12

    ##Agents & tooling4 stories

    Anthropic builds Scion to replace Git after merged changes grew from 2,500 to over 30,000 a day

    Willison reports Anthropic's engineering post: merged code changes averaged around 2,500 a day in May 2026 and passed 30,000 a day in September. The main branch history alone is almost 100 GB, and CI runs about 25 million checkouts a day. A team of seven finished the migration from Git in five months, and Anthropic credits Google's Piper and Meta's Sapling as prior art. Zvi's AI #190 calls Scion Anthropic's internal replacement for GitHub.

    Drew Breunig names a new context failure mode, Context Inertia

    It is when a model commits to a hypothesis or subtask and keeps pursuing it after the evidence says to stop. He points to Anthropic's Memento harness, where each agent writes a short note after a fixed timeout and a fresh agent takes over with only the note.

    Drew Breunig · Wed 14

    Geoffrey Huntley fixes agent drift with a pre-commit hook

    When an agent skips his diagnostics library, he fails the commit and injects a prompt telling it to use the library. He says that took the agent to ten out of ten, which updating AGENTS.md could not guarantee.

    Simon Willison · Tue 13

    Willison builds web-edit to change files on a remote machine from his phone

    He had Codex and GPT-6 Astra Extra High write a Python web app for viewing and editing files in a folder, meant to run over Tailscale. He wanted to edit a credentials file without sending it through the model.

    Simon Willison · Mon 12

    Also Python 3.15 is out, and Willison singles out explicit lazy imports from PEP 810 and drops support for 3.10 · Addy Osmani and colleagues, quoted by Willison, say review on the Claude Code team now runs continuously and Claude has reviewed most changes before a human looks · Lean Rada's document.currentScript trick replaces an inline element with a value when the page loads.

    ##Security4 stories

    Anthropic gives METR employee-like access and names SecureBio and Accenture as evaluators

    Anthropic has onboarded six METR employees with company hardware, office space and internal tools roughly on par with its own risk assessment teams. METR will publish its analysis of Anthropic's risk levels, incidents and practices, and Anthropic cannot veto it but can require redactions in predefined categories. Anthropic is also working with SecureBio and Accenture, and is in early talks with others including Hugging Face.

    Anthropic · Thu 15

    • Anthropic: an update on independent evaluatorsanthropic.com

    Zvi covers OpenAI firing three safety researchers

    Per Zvi, Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni and Jasmine Wang were let go for what OpenAI called mishandling sensitive company information. The three published an open letter saying they believe the reasons were pretextual, and OpenAI says there was a further breach of trust that it did not detail.

    Zvi · Mon 12

    Zvi calls a leaked Anthropic draft AI bill unacceptable

    He describes it as similar to the FRONTIER Act with stronger preemption of state laws, stronger transparency and weaker enforcement. He says he has not yet read the full bill and that Anthropic is purportedly shopping it around.

    Zvi · Sat 17

    Goodfire argues neural networks are already clean

    Latent Space leads with Nick Cammarata's essay claiming that many known cases of polysemanticity and superposition give way to further agentic analysis. It calls the piece a rare story on interpretability and alignment.

    Latent Space · Fri 16

    Also Zvi's AI #190 reports a group used ChatGPT to build a full-chain Chrome exploit and claimed a $250,000 bounty · Anthropic's midterms post samples about 250,000 US Claude.ai conversations: 5.75% touch politics, 0.51% are about elections, and about 0.01% ask whom to vote for.

    ##Money & compute2 stories

    Anthropic tells investors its third quarter revenue was $16.8bn, up from $11.5bn

    Willison summarises the FT report: revenue was about $4.8bn in the first quarter, $11.5bn in the second and $16.8bn in the third. The third quarter brought $1.70 of revenue for every $1 spent on computing power, with an adjusted operating margin of 11% before stock-based pay. The FT also says distillation by other labs made up 5% to 7% of revenue and was cut back materially in the third quarter. Willison notes Anthropic no longer seems to lean on annualized run-rate revenue with investors.

    Simon Willison · Thu 15

    • Willison: Anthropic tells investors quarterly revenue is up nearly 50%simonwillison.net

    Modal raises a $750M Series D at a $15.75B valuation

    Modal says it has passed $1B in annualized run rate, and the round was led by Accel. Latent Space frames the sandbox and agent infrastructure business as an infra decacorn.

    Latent Space · Thu 15

    Also Latent Space's Muse Wonder digest lists Artificial Analysis cost per index task: $4.52 for Wonder, $7.63 for Fable 5.1, $5.98 for Opus 5.5, $3.26 for Astra and $1.99 for Argon, and says Meta stock rose 3% on launch day.

    ##Opinion3 columns

    Nathan Lambert · Sat 17

    US open weights are back

    Lambert calls Meta's Muse Spark Open the strongest American-made open model by quite some margin. With Reflection's Beam, he says the gap between the best American and Chinese models has roughly halved, though 20+ Chinese open-weight models would otherwise beat the best Western ones.

    Meta shakes up the frontiers, open and closed

    Zvi · Wed 14

    The safety culture at the labs is broken

    Zvi says Anthropic cultivates a safety culture, but that much of the company relies on muddling through. He argues OpenAI's problems run deeper and points to a growing view that the frontier needs pacing.

    The Safety Culture at the Labs is Broken

    Popcar via Simon Willison · Wed 14

    AI decompilation is awesome and scary

    Popcar describes a wave of AI decompilation that enables game mashups and a boom in preservation, often running in a browser through WebAssembly. He warns that anyone can decompile software, so a paid app can be open-sourced against its owner's will.

    AI is mass decompiling games. That's both awesome and scary.
