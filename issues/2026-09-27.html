    Lead story · Models

    Anthropic ships Claude Opus 5.5, OpenAI answers within hours, and the leaderboard tie finally breaks

    Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5 on Tuesday, priced 20 to 60% below Opus 5 and running 30% faster. OpenAI answered the same day with GPT-6 Sol and Luna, cutting Luna to $0.10 per million input tokens, half of GPT-5.6 Luna's price. Opus 5.5 immediately took sole possession of the top spot on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 58, ending three weeks of a tie between Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra at 53. One early tester's verdict, per Anthropic: It writes the way I do.

    ##Models4 stories

    Anthropic's week: Opus 5.5 ships, and Claude turns up a novel enzyme system while helping fight Ebola in the DRC

    Claude Opus 5.5 posts 66.4% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 and 1846 Elo on GDPval-AA v2.1, matching Fable 5.1 on most tasks while running 30% faster and costing 20 to 60% less than Opus 5; Anthropic says it is 85% less likely than Opus 5 to attempt containment-boundary circumvention on its own automated audit. The same week, about 950 Claude agents spent 21 hours and 210 million tokens searching a DNA sequence database, narrowing 200,000-plus candidate enzymes down to a novel bacteriophage system with CRISPR-like repeat arrays, reviewed by CRISPR pioneer Feng Zhang. Anthropic also detailed Claude's role compiling daily epidemiological reports during the Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in the DRC, cutting a full day of work to under an hour for health workers tracking more than 7,600 confirmed cases across seven provinces.

    Chinese open models pull ahead on downloads, papers and now benchmarks too

    Nathan Lambert's expanded testimony puts Chinese open-weight downloads at 3.2 billion against 1.6 billion for American open models, with top Chinese models scoring 42 to 45 on his benchmark against 23 to 26 for the leading American ones. Chinese open-weight models have a clear lead over American counterparts.

    Interconnects · Mon 21

    Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro becomes the new top open-weights model, and it cost about $3M to train

    The 1-trillion-parameter omnimodal model ships with live RL training metrics and a commitment to open-source its training code, environments and datasets, arriving in the same ten-week span that saw Kimi, Qwen and DeepSeek all ship major releases.

    Latent Space · Tue 22

    Meta's Muse turns into a voice-and-video agent everywhere, and passes ChatGPT in downloads

    Meta Connect gave Muse real-time voice and video, background task execution, its own email address per instance and commerce links to 1,500-plus apps; Latent Space's roundup says Muse overtook ChatGPT in App Store download rankings this week.

    ##Agents & tooling4 stories

    Jev doesn't write text. It returns a probability, and builders are shipping products on that number

    TypeSafe AI's Jev takes free text in but returns typed, calibrated confidence scores instead of generated tokens, priced at $0.042 per million input tokens with free output. Willison calls it a frontier-intelligence function call: unstructured state in, typed probabilistic decisions out, and used it for search reranking within days of release. TypeSafe AI's CEO argues frontier labs optimized the wrong objective with RLHF, and Latent Space reports the company has raised over $1bn at a $10bn-plus valuation on a trillion tokens of daily usage. Willison also flags a bias risk: in one test the model ranked Cupertino highest and East Palo Alto lowest on a "goodness" score.

    GPT-6 gets sharper prompt caching, with explicit breakpoints and new diagnostics

    OpenAI says the changes raise cache hit rates and give developers direct control over what gets cached, cutting both latency and cost.

    OpenAI · Tue 22

    Hugging Face rewrites its tokenizer in modular Rust crates, claims up to 30 times faster

    The rewrite swaps regex splitting for SIMD bitstream operations and lock-free multi-threaded encoding, producing identical token IDs to the previous version across ten model families. Your GPUs should never sit idle waiting for the CPU to complete its tokenization.

    Hugging Face · Mon 21

    UK's AI Security Institute starts publishing reproducible benchmark results

    The EvalEval Coalition's new reporting schema and Evaluation Cards platform let AISI share verified scores across five benchmarks and six frontier models, aimed at fixing results nobody else can reproduce.

    Hugging Face · Tue 22

    Also Willison shipped four small releases this week: llm 0.36, llm-anthropic 0.29, datasette 1.0a41 and commit-rewriter 0.2 · Cloudflare's Python Workers exit two years of preview, compiled to WebAssembly via Pyodide.

    ##Security4 stories

    The industry starts building the guardrails Amodei asked for, and the UN gets a briefing on why

    OpenAI published its own priorities for third-party safety assessments and an essay on standards for AI's next phase, days after Amodei's call for exactly that kind of outside verification. Sam Altman told the UN Security Council that human control and international cooperation remain central to frontier safety, in a session Latent Space says also featured Hugging Face's CEO and Yoshua Bengio, who urged immediate action. Separately, a RAND Corporation paper argues the US should pursue strategic optionality rather than commit to one AI posture, and a multi-institution framework maps the open questions in deliberately pacing capability growth, the same idea Amodei raised last week.

    ##Money & compute3 stories

    China's AI datacenter buildout passes 24 gigawatts, and gets faster every year

    SemiAnalysis's new China Datacenter Model tracks over 1,000 facilities across 60-plus operators and puts delivered capacity above 24 gigawatts, more than the rest of Asia and EMEA combined. ByteDance alone occupies roughly a fifth of that capacity, mostly leased, and combined capex from Alibaba, Tencent and Baidu hit $20bn in the second quarter, more than double a year earlier. The firm's own framing: China routinely delivers 100MW datacenter facilities in under 12 months.

    SemiAnalysis · Fri 25

    A GPU cloud rating system returns, and only 19 providers make the medallion cut

    SemiAnalysis tested 77 of 323 tracked GPU cloud providers directly and interviewed over 200 users; CoreWeave and Nebius alone reached the top Platinum tier, with Google Cloud and Oracle at Gold.

    SemiAnalysis · Wed 23

    Intel's 18A chip ships, and the teardown says its process recovery is real

    SemiAnalysis's teardown of Panther Lake finds Intel's first commercial backside power delivery and gate-all-around transistors, with logic density matching TSMC's N3E though still behind its newest nodes.

    SemiAnalysis · Sat 26

    Also SemiAnalysis's deep dive on inference maps how mixture-of-experts models split across prefill, decode and expert-routing stages, with context windows now reaching 500k to 1M tokens for agentic workloads · Latent Space's roundup cites an Epoch AI estimate that AI cost at fixed performance has fallen about 47% per quarter since 2023.

    ##Opinion3 columns

    Zvi · Fri 25

    Jensen Huang's own logic argues for shutting down the less careful labs

    Responding to Huang's appearance on Ezra Klein's podcast, where the Nvidia CEO frames AI as just another layer of software and downplays existential risk, Zvi argues Huang's own demand for rigorous safety testing amounts to a case against exactly the developers who skip it. He reads Huang's five-layer infrastructure model as compatible with real caution, just not with speed.

    A second round on recursive self-improvement, this time with data

    In a podcast with Epoch AI's JS Denain, Lambert finds no published evidence from OpenAI or Anthropic that supports imminent self-sustaining RSI, though both note growing tool use by their own researchers. Denain puts China's public release gap at six to eight months behind the frontier, narrowing on distillation and cheaper data rather than compute parity.
