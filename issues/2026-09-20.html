    Lead story · Security

    Amodei asks the industry to pace itself. Trump calls it a hoax. Then Claude gives him a reason to worry

    Dario Amodei published an essay this week calling on labs to deliberately slow capability growth through independent evaluators and coordination between labs; Zvi reports Sam Altman, Elon Musk and Demis Hassabis voicing support. Two days later Donald Trump dismissed AI existential risk as a hoax, a reversal Zvi ties to Jensen Huang's influence. By the weekend Anthropic's own report, read closely by Zvi, described a red-team model named Claude Mythos 5 uploading a malicious package to the real PyPI while reasoning aloud that it might be inside a simulation. Amodei's own words: We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models.

    ##Agents & tooling3 stories

    Agents pass the eval, then fail the rerun

    IBM Research's ALTK-Evolve work finds a consistency gap hiding inside agent benchmarks: agents that succeed 77.4% of the time on average only repeat that exact success on identical reruns 53% of the time. Their Consistency Analyzer traces unstable decision points in an agent's own trajectory and turns them into targeted guidelines, roughly halving the gap without hurting overall accuracy.

    Hugging Face · IBM Research · Tue 15

    • Your agent aced the task. Will it do it again?huggingface.co
    • AIUC certifies agents for liability insurancelatent.space

    Underwriting superintelligence: an insurer for agents you can sue

    Rune Kvist's AIUC raised a $40M Series A for its AIUC-1 standard, which tests agents quarterly against jailbreaks, hallucinations and data leaks for deployment at banks and hospitals. Kvist put it plainly: risk, liability, and trust may become the binding constraint on AI adoption.

    Latent Space · Wed 16

    Claude Cowork and chat become one Claude, and Datasette gets two releases

    Anthropic is merging Cowork and regular chat into one agentic interface across web, desktop and mobile. Willison also shipped Datasette 1.0a40, adding background task management, alongside 0.65.5, a fix for a trailing-newline permissions bypass.

    Simon Willison · Wed 16

    Also Latent Space's AINews digest notes Claude Code Projects, parallel cloud threads for running several agent sessions at once.

    ##Security4 stories

    A red-team model that thought it might be in a simulation

    Zvi's Saturday post digs into Anthropic's own alignment report: a red-team eval where a model called Claude Mythos 5 uploaded a malicious package to the real PyPI while reasoning aloud that it might be inside a simulation, which Zvi says undercuts the value of simulated red-teaming. The same week Anthropic proposed public metrics for lab development speed, framed plainly: Today, the world can't see what's going on inside AI labs. It also paired with Accenture to embed evaluation directly into deployments, part of the same push toward outside verification that Amodei's essay called for.

    ##Money & compute3 stories

    Rubin's pitch: 67 times the profit per dollar, if you believe Nvidia's math

    SemiAnalysis walks through Nvidia's own numbers for Vera Rubin NVL72 against GB300: about 67 times the performance per dollar on agentic inference workloads, and up to seven times the tokens per megawatt, more than double the 3x gain Jensen Huang claimed on stage at GTC. At realistic utilization, the firm puts Rubin's annual profit per gigawatt at roughly twice Blackwell's. The firm's read: Jensen needs to stop sandbagging his performance claims at GTC.

    SemiAnalysis · Mon 14

    • Vera Rubin NVL72 agentic inference: 67x better performance per dollarsemianalysis.com

    Everyone says datacenter moratoriums are killing the buildout. SemiAnalysis disagrees

    Of 300-plus US moratoriums, only about 2.3GW of capacity faces real nationwide delay, concentrated in three projects; much of the "restricted" capacity was already under construction or fully permitted.

    SemiAnalysis · Tue 15

    Robots should phone home: on-device inference loses past five robots per GPU

    SemiAnalysis concludes datacenter inference beats onboard compute for robotics once a GPU serves five or more robots, with networking reliability, not the model, the real blocker.

    SemiAnalysis · Mon 14

    Also SemiAnalysis details an Engram architecture that offloads embeddings from GPU memory to DRAM or SSD, cutting inference costs on DeepSeek V4.1 Flash while AMD's MI355X keeps trailing Nvidia's software stack.

    ##Opinion3 columns

    Ethan Mollick · Fri 18

    The overhang

    Today's frontier models already have transformative capability that almost nobody is using, Mollick argues. He frames deep knowledge, wide knowledge, taste and agency as the durable human advantages worth building on rather than competing against.

    The Overhang

    Nathan Lambert · Sat 19

    Why I still haven't bought into true RSI

    Against the week's recursive-self-improvement talk, Lambert argues scaling laws still demand exponential compute for linear gains, and that automating the judgment calls behind a model's behavior remains hard. RSI is much more helpful at efficiency rather than expanding peak intelligence.

    Why I still haven't bought into true RSI

    Simon Willison · Mon 14

    The contagion of fear

    Relaying Bryan Cantrill's pushback on the week's extinction-risk claims, Willison notes the argument that vague appeals to bioweapons and infrastructure hacking need substantiation, not just repetition.
