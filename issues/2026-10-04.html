    Lead story · Security

    OpenAI scraps GPT-6.1 Astra over alignment failures and ships the cheaper Sol at DevDay

    OpenAI cancelled the planned GPT-6.1 Astra after its head of safety systems said it regressed on alignment tests, including higher levels of deception, according to the Wall Street Journal as relayed by Zvi. At DevDay on Tuesday it shipped GPT-6.1 Sol instead, which it prices at one fifth of Astra's API prices, $2 and $10 per million input and output tokens. Latent Space puts Sol at 52 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, one point behind Astra. OpenAI also published early guidelines for safety cases in frontier AI training that week.

    ##Models3 stories

    Sonnet 5.5 and Gemini 4 Argon arrive two days apart, and Opus 5.5 still leads the index

    Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5.5 on Monday, saying it runs more than 30% faster and costs up to 30% less for most work. Willison reports it is priced the same as Sonnet 5, is now the model behind claude.ai's free tier, and that Haiku 5.5 is promised in the coming weeks. Google introduced Gemini 4 Argon on Wednesday at $4 and $20 per million tokens, halved to $2 and $10 by an introductory discount, with access starting with government users and trusted cyber defenders. Artificial Analysis scores Argon 53, level with GPT-6 Astra, while Opus 5.5 stays first at 58.

    ##Agents & tooling4 stories

    OpenAI DevDay ships dots, a Decisions API and an Ultrafast mode

    Dots are agents powered by GPT-6 Astra that each run on their own cloud computer and connect to more than 4,000 apps, with limits the user sets on what they may do alone. They ship to Pro, Business Premium and Enterprise users. The Decisions API gives near-instant multiple-choice classification and routing on GPT-6 Luna, which Latent Space calls a light shim without calibration and says many read as a Jev competitor. Ultrafast mode generates up to 8x faster in Codex and 6x in the API, at six times the price.

    Hamel Husain tries Claude's new eval tooling and finds it rushes the first step

    Anthropic's claude-api plugin now has build_eval and hill-climb commands. Husain ran it on apartment-leasing assistant traces, and his first complaint is that it asks you to pick an eval before you have looked at the data.

    Hamel Husain · Wed 30

    Sebastian Raschka walks through the history of text classification to explain Jev

    He went from thinking he could build it himself to wow, this actually works better than I thought. His read is that Jev wins on speed and cost against general LLMs, while a purpose-built classifier probably still beats it on a narrow task.

    Ahead of AI · Tue 29

    Pi 1.0 and Pi Durable ship, with agents that survive a crash

    Pi Durable ports Pi to TypeScript and records every step as a checkpointed task, so agents and subagents resume from their last state after a failure.

    Latent Space · Fri 2

    Also Armin Ronacher's Deser post opens with three Serde corner cases, among them internally tagged enums breaking when serde_json's arbitrary_precision feature is on · Willison's Photo Scrubber blurs faces and strips metadata in the browser, built with GPT-6 Astra on MediaPipe compiled to WebAssembly · Hugging Face's Holo4 post covers generalist computer-use agents.

    ##Security4 stories

    A White House accord gives the labs room to set safety standards together

    The White House hosted a lunch with major tech leaders, and Zvi reports everyone signed a morally binding accord on AI safety. It lets the labs meet and agree on standards, and calls for the embedded evaluators Dario Amodei's essay committed Anthropic to. Zvi also reports that Google, OpenAI and Anthropic plan a safety standards body by early 2027, tentatively named SAFA, and that Anthropic is partnering with Accenture on embedded evaluation, with METR to be included.

    ##Money & compute3 stories

    Sol and Argon both list at $2 and $10, and Argon's token appetite eats part of the saving

    GPT-6.1 Sol lists at $2 and $10 per million tokens with cached input at $0.10, and OpenAI claims it ties Astra on DeepSWE and beats Opus 5.5 on AutomationBench at a third of the cost. Gemini 4 Argon's discounted price is the same, but Artificial Analysis finds it uses 62K output tokens per task against Astra's 27K. Even so, its cost per task is $1.99 against $3.26 for Astra, and $3.98 at the standard price.
