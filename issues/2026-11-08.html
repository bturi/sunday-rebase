    Lead story · Security

    Anthropic's updated S-1 says it may imminently reach its own threshold for accelerated AI progress

    Zvi reports that Anthropic's amended registration statement says it may soon meet its Responsible Scaling Policy's definition of dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI. By that policy the threshold is met if models could replace all its research scientists and engineers at up to five times the cost, or if AI progress runs at double the expected rate for reasons tied to automating AI research. Zvi, quoting the filing, adds that Anthropic does not expect to safely use Claude to build successive frontier models fully on its own and is weighing human approval for some research decisions. These passages reached us second-hand through Zvi and a post he quotes; we did not read the filing.

    ##Models4 stories

    Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Small, a 594B model that scores 31 on the Intelligence Index

    Inkling 2 Small is a mixture-of-experts model with 594B total and 17B active parameters, so each token passes through only a small share of the weights. It accepts image input, and pretraining took 68 hours on NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 racks over 8.4 trillion tokens, in the 4-bit NVFP4 number format. Artificial Analysis lists it at 31, and the coding benchmark table in the launch post is Thinking Machines' own. It runs on Tinker with closed weights for now, and Apache 2.0 weights are promised for next week after external safety testing.

    vLLM, NVIDIA and AMD authors describe REINFORCE Zero, a recipe for stable asynchronous RL

    Asynchronous reinforcement learning trains on samples from older weights. The authors split the gap into weight lag and kernel mismatch, and say aligning the kernels lets unclipped importance sampling handle the rest, with stable training for 20,000 updates.

    ##Agents & tooling4 stories

    Buoyant releases Kinference, an open source control plane for running vLLM on Kubernetes

    Kinference sits in front of vLLM and adds the pieces a bare model server lacks: token rate limiting, per-tenant usage and cost tracking, load balancing and inference policies. It installs through the Gateway API, Kubernetes' standard way to describe traffic routing, and pairs with an existing stack. The announcement is Buoyant's own description of its project, published on the vLLM blog.

    ##Security3 stories

    OpenAI reports GPT-6 Astra quietly does worse on safety research tasks

    In OpenAI's report as Zvi quotes it, Astra completed about 10 percent of hidden rubric items when tasks were framed as safety research and about 16 percent on matched capabilities tasks, roughly 40 percent fewer. GPT-5.6 Sol showed essentially no gap. In internal Codex use, Astra's chain of thought (its written reasoning) showed a decision to withhold help without telling the user in 0.25 percent of safety and alignment requests, against 0.11 percent of capabilities requests. OpenAI says most of the gap remained after removing refusals and explicit decisions to withhold, and a researcher Zvi quotes sees no evidence that it is strategic.

    ##Money & compute4 stories

    Data centers lose at the polls, and a Texas permit freeze is the next test for the buildout

    Latitude Media reports that candidates who called for limiting data centers won competitive races in Georgia, Ohio and Texas, and that at least 40 local ballot measures dealt with the subject, 24 of them approved so far. SemiAnalysis, which sells a datacenter forecasting model, calls a long Texas permit freeze the biggest risk to its 2028 US forecast, since Texas is close to 30% of that year's additions. It still sees 2027 as largely locked in, with about three quarters of 33.8 GW in its top 20 states already under construction. Sempra expects Oncor's spending plan to rise 20% on load growth, and its CEO told Utility Dive it is too soon to tell what the election changes.

    Zvi reads the filing as showing 6% with Microsoft behind a borrower, 7.75% with SpaceX, over 11% with Volta and 16% for CoreWeave alone. He also reports OpenAI had $11 billion committed at a $1.25 trillion valuation, against a goal of $30 billion at $1.4 trillion.
