    Lead story · Security

    PHP patches two critical remote code execution flaws after the disclosure leaks

    The PHP project released fixes on Wednesday for two remote code execution flaws that affect the whole 8.x line, moving the release up a day after CVE details began circulating on Tuesday. Willison says exploits were circulating by the night before the patch, after a security firm mistakenly published a working proof of concept video. Latent Space says OpenAI's Patch the Planet program was one of two parties to find the bugs, which it calls PHP2Shell. Zvi's AI #191 adds a GitLab flaw rated CVSS 10.0 and a Railway breach from the same days.

    ##Models4 stories

    Moonshot releases Kimi K3.1 Pro at $2 and $10 per million tokens, with open weights promised by 4 November

    Moonshot made Kimi K3.1 Pro available through its API this week and says full weights will follow by 4 November. Willison lists $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, with a Highspeed version that runs 2 to 3 times faster at twice the price. Latent Space gives 2.8T total and 104B active parameters with a 1M token context. Artificial Analysis lists it at 55, behind Claude Opus 5.5 at 58.

    DeepSeek releases V4.1 Flash 1017, a 552B model with 16B active parameters under the MIT license

    Interconnects says it accepts image inputs and does well on the agentic coding benchmark DeepSWE at a low price. The same roundup picks Reflection's Beam as the strongest permissively licensed American open model.

    Interconnects · Tue 20

    Jack Clark tells the IMF the singularity has begun

    His Import AI 477 is a transcript of a Bangkok speech that cites Humanity's Last Exam at 67.7% for Claude Opus 5.5, up from 8% in January 2025. He also says Anthropic's researchers produce almost 10 times as much code as in the company's first four years.

    Jack Clark · Mon 19

    Ankit Maloo on why models solve some hard math problems

    He argues that models search many branches cheaply and use automated verifiers to prune dead ends, where humans go deep on a few ideas. Latent Space's essay cites a reported Navier-Stokes proof that took 10,000 agents, about 300B tokens and 88 hours.

    Latent Space · Fri 23

    Also The Interconnects roundup also lists Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro-RL and NVIDIA's Nemotron-3.5-Super-VL-120B-A12B · IBM's Granite Speech 5.0 is on Hugging Face · Zvi's AI #191 lists Opus 5.5 fast mode at $8 and $40 per million tokens.

    ##Agents & tooling4 stories

    Anthropic donates the Agent Skills standard to the Agentic AI Foundation

    Anthropic is handing over the Agent Skills name, logo, GitHub organization and agentskills.io domain to the Agentic AI Foundation, which sits under the Linux Foundation and also hosts MCP. It says more than 70 agent platforms have integrated the standard, with 1 million skills published and nearly 280 million installed. David Soria Parra and Mahesh Murag are lead maintainers, and versioned releases, a proposal process and security guidance for skill authors are planned. Willison notes he predicted the move in December.

    Anthropic · Simon Willison · Thu 22

    • Anthropic: donating Agent Skills to the Agentic AI Foundationanthropic.com
    • Willison: donating Agent Skillssimonwillison.net

    Dan McAteer describes Claude Mods, functions that change how Claude Code behaves and looks

    They run inside Claude Code and respond to events such as prompts and tool calls. Mods are not sandboxed and run with the user's permissions, and a supervisor mod he built fired on 99 of 111 turns.

    Latent Space · Wed 21

    Hamel Husain compares decision models as cheaper stand-ins for LLM judges

    He covers Jev, Perplexity Decider, Liquid d1, Cloudflare Clef Flash and OpenAI Decisions for pass/fail evals. He says to tune each model's threshold instead of keeping the default of 0.50.

    Hamel Husain · Tue 20

    Drew Breunig says context engineering is shifting toward deterministic harness code

    His write-up of an AGNTCon talk says the core fixes for context failures have stayed largely the same. The central task, he argues, is deciding which parts to make deterministic and which to leave to the model.

    Drew Breunig · Sat 24

    Also Willison's Battle Drones is a browser RTS built through twelve major iterations with coding agents, with audio-based multiplayer · OpenAI's Decisions API is a low-cost API for fast, focused decisions · Hugging Face covers MPS in PyTorch 2.15 and AnyLanguageModel 1.0 for local models on Apple platforms.

    ##Security4 stories

    Eight eMudhra intermediate CA keys are compromised, and uv, pip and certifi ship new certificate lists

    Willison reports that the private keys of eight eMudhra intermediate CA certificates have been compromised. The intermediates chain to roots trusted by the Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple and Chrome root programs, so anything that bundles a trusted certificate list needs updating. Releases so far include uv 0.13.4, pip 26.2.2 and certifi 2026.10.19, and Linux distributions need to update their ca-certificates packages.

    Simon Willison · Tue 20

    • Willison: eMudhra intermediate CA compromisesimonwillison.net

    Zvi warns of a wave of AI-driven attacks over the next 6 to 12 months

    He cites a report of AI agents compromising more than 100 companies after about 1,200 attempts, at roughly $5,000 in tokens and about 23 hours per successful intrusion. He also notes that 46.1% of the top 7.2M PHP sites that show a version run an end-of-life branch.

    Zvi · Fri 23

    Zvi reports an Anthropic Fellows paper on transcoder features that are neurons in disguise

    He ties it to a wider claim that individual neurons mostly carry single, labelable meanings, which he says lowers the estimated difficulty of interpretability work.

    Zvi · Thu 22

    DHS bars DeepSeek from federal civilian procurement, and China floats pre-approval for frontier model exports

    Interconnects says the Chinese proposal is not yet law and appears to give open-source technologies automatic approval. Its authors call the reaction to it overblown and the DHS order expected.

    Interconnects · Tue 20

    Also OpenAI describes stronger containment for frontier model training, and says Cloudflare uses its Daybreak to investigate vulnerabilities and propose fixes · OpenAI's Patch the Planet post frames the work as connecting researchers, maintainers and responders.

    ##Money & compute3 stories

    DeepSeek open sources DeepInfer and reports about 155 trillion tokens a day from one cluster

    Latent Space says the release covers the inference engine and API layer, built mainly on vLLM under Apache 2.0, and leaves out the KV-cache and load-balancing parts, so the production service cannot be fully rebuilt from it. DeepSeek's own unaudited figures include a 96% input-cache hit rate, which means much of those tokens are cached input, and $38 of revenue per accelerator hour at off-peak prices. The roughly 80% margin and 81 day payback are Latent Space's estimates. Interconnects calls DeepInfer one of the bigger open source AI artifacts of recent months.

    Latent Space · Interconnects · Tue 20 to Wed 21

    • Latent Space: DeepSeek DeepInfer digestlatent.space
    • Interconnects: latest open artifacts #25interconnects.ai

    Anthropic sets up a public benefit fund and plans to commit 5% of its shares

    The fund is an LLC, and the board may add 2% more for each 50% increase in company value above a baseline. Anthropic says none of its equity may go to lobbying or political giving.

    Anthropic · Sun 18

    ##Opinion3 columns

    Ethan Mollick · Tue 20

    30 months on the exponential

    Marking his new book Co-Existence, Mollick counts 180 models that now beat GPT-4 at its release, 19 of which run on a smartphone. He says the length of task AI completes at 50% reliability grew from about four minutes in 2024 to 17 hours by April 2026.

    30 Months on the Exponential (in Charts)

    Jeremy Scheff via Simon Willison · Mon 19

    My obituary for programming

    Scheff says programming paid well because programmers solved logic puzzles others could not, and coding agents take that role away. Willison adds that the loss is largest for developers who enjoyed solving the problems themselves.

    My obituary for programming

    Armin Ronacher via Simon Willison · Thu 22

    The industry's old software debt comes due

    Ronacher says the software industry built a lot of poor software over the past three decades. He suggests the trouble now arriving with AI is a consequence of that.

    Why AI compute comes in rectangles: Erik Bernhardsson of Modal

    He argues that fixed GPU contracts do not fit demand that grows, spikes and falls, so Modal pools demand across thousands of customers and rents from about 25 clouds. Latent Space notes that locking in capacity at today's prices risks losses if GPU prices fall.
