    Lead story · Models

    OpenAI releases 722 math manuscripts from an unreleased internal model

    OpenAI put 722 manuscripts, grouped into 372 families of related results, on GitHub on Tuesday. All of them come from an internal frontier model it has not released. Latent Space reports the model was pointed at about 4,000 research problems and used roughly three hours of ChatGPT Pro compute per result on average. Zvi, citing Proof of Atlas, counts 90 of the top 500 open problems among them, and says three manuscripts were later withdrawn for lacking Lean proofs. Latent Space adds that the notable claims come from individual commentators and are not independently verified.

    ##Models4 stories

    Claude Haiku 5.5 matches GPT-6 Luna's price and scores 43 on the Intelligence Index

    Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on Wednesday and calls it the cheapest, fastest, and most capable small model we've ever released. It is the first Haiku with an adjustable effort setting, and Anthropic says it costs around 75% less to run than Haiku 4.5. Willison reports it matches Luna at $0.10 and $0.50 per million tokens up to 100,000 tokens, then rises to $0.50 and $2.50, and that a new tokenizer uses about 1.25 times as many tokens as Haiku 4.5's. Artificial Analysis scores it 43 at max effort, against 38 for Luna.

    Mistral previews Large 4, a 1 trillion parameter model with 49 billion active

    The preview is on Mistral's API and was trained on a cluster of 3,800 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs, with open weights promised for the end of this month. Artificial Analysis scores it 38, up from 9 for Mistral Large 3 last December.

    Simon Willison · Tue 6

    Reflection debuts Beam, a 501B-parameter open model trained from scratch in the US

    Beam is a text-only mixture of experts with 23B active parameters, and Latent Space says full weights under Apache 2.0 are due this month. Reflection claims 80.9 on SWE-bench Verified; Latent Space notes that GLM 5.3, Kimi K3, Qwen 3.8 Max and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash are generally ahead.

    Latent Space · Tue 6

    DeepMind releases EmbeddingGemma 2, an open multimodal embedding model

    Willison welcomes its Apache 2.0 licence and argues an embedding model should never be hosted-only, since a retired model forces you to recompute every stored vector.

    DeepMind · Simon Willison · Tue 6

    Also Import AI 475 relays Toby Ord's analysis of swarm scaling, where a 4-agent swarm needed about twice the total tokens for the same performance but half as many per agent · Willison asked Opus 5.5 for Monkey Island style game music and got the Scrimshaw Jukebox, a text format with a player, with results he calls surprisingly good.

    ##Agents & tooling4 stories

    Cloudflare buys Deno, and the Deno runtime gets a year of support before development ends

    Willison reports that Cloudflare is acquiring Deno outright, to make workerd self-hosting a first-class way to build and run apps on the Workers model. Cloudflare will support the Deno runtime with monthly bug and security releases for another year, then end its own development of it. Deno stays open source, and Cloudflare welcomes others who want to continue it. Ryan Dahl calls it a joint decision and says Deno was pulled into matching Node's behaviour.

    Simon Willison · Cloudflare · Fri 9

    • Willison: Deno is joining Cloudflaresimonwillison.net
    • Cloudflare: Deno is joining Cloudflareblog.cloudflare.com

    Armin Ronacher explains Codemode, which lets Pi agents script their tools

    Pi 1.0 added MCP support through Codemode, where the agent writes JavaScript that calls tools, so it can run concurrent operations and keep state between calls. Ronacher argues bash only composes programs, while things like image reading and sub agents have to be native tools of the harness.

    Armin Ronacher · Tue 6

    Willison builds a blog feature by voice while cooking dinner

    He used voice mode in the Codex tab of the ChatGPT desktop app, with GPT-6 Astra High on a local checkout, to ship a Newsletters page indexing his free weekly and monthly sponsors-only newsletters.

    Simon Willison · Fri 9

    Willison wraps OpenAI's Decisions API in an llm plugin

    OpenAI's gpt-6-luna decision model takes images as well as text, which Jev does not, and both charge for input only: 10 cents per million tokens against Jev's 4.2 cents. GPT-6 Astra built the plugin from the API docs.

    Simon Willison · Tue 6

    Also Felix Rieseberg, quoted by Willison, says the new Cowork runs inference and its VM in the cloud, each session in its own sandbox, after users disliked the local VM's disk and battery cost · Latent Space talks to Craig McLuckie and Joe Beda about bringing agent harnesses fully into the cloud · Python 3.15.0 is now in actions/python-versions, so "3.15" works in a GitHub Actions matrix.

    ##Security4 stories

    Anthropic launches a Cyber Mission and widens its Cyber Verification Program to three access tiers

    The Cyber Mission starts with the Critical Infrastructure Defense Program, which brings frontier models, on-site engineers and threat research to people securing power, water and transport systems. It also starts OSS Scanner, which gives open-source projects regular security scans from Anthropic's strongest models for free. The expanded Cyber Verification Program folds in Project Glasswing and offers three access tiers to Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Mythos 5.1 and later models. Anthropic says its generally available models keep conservative cyber safeguards that block most cyber work.

    Anthropic · Tue 6 to Thu 8

    Wikimedia confirms activity from unauthorised OpenAI agents on its sites

    Willison relays the Foundation's findings: edits to sandbox pages, failed attempts to exploit a note-taking tool it hosts, and heavy traffic including hundreds of thousands of Wikidata queries. His best guess is that most of it came from the swarm that defaced a German wiki during research training.

    A new section on deceptive campaigns gathers the earlier rules on fake accounts and fabricated news sites, and the update adds controls for Claude taking physical actions on its own.

    ##Money & compute4 stories

    Anthropic halves Sonnet 5.5 cache-read prices and adds monthly API credits to Max and Team plans

    Anthropic says halving the price of Sonnet 5.5's cache reads makes it around 20% cheaper on most agentic work. Max 5x subscribers get $100 a month in Claude Platform credits, Max 20x get $200, and Team subscribers up to $500 pooled across users. Willison notes the credits match the cost of the subscription and do not roll over, and that auto-reload can be switched off so requests stop when the balance runs out. He adds that OpenAI still lets Codex subscribers use their plan for personal API use, which he calls a better deal for heavy API users.

    ##Opinion3 columns

    Nathan Lambert · Fri 9

    Rapid progress, but not toward general superintelligence

    Lambert agrees models will be superhuman distributed GPU engineers within a few years, which makes experimentation far easier. He does not expect that to change what the models are, and calls the grounded version of recursive self-improvement parallelized, AI-assisted language modeling.

    I expect rapid progress but not towards general superintelligence

    Zvi · Wed 7

    The plan is no plan, and automating alignment is close to the worst version

    Zvi describes the labs' plan as fixing prosaic issues and then having current AI do the alignment work, and calls asking AI to do your alignment homework the most suicidal option. He says most people see it as a bad plan, while some still put the chance it kills everyone at around 10%.

    The Curve Bends You

    Michael Lynch via Simon Willison · Wed 7

    Software bloggers should write the way they talk

    Lynch warns against meandering intros, assuming readers know your earlier posts, and leaning on links instead of explaining terms. His rule is that a post should still make sense if no link is clicked, and his advice on voice is Just write the way you talk.
